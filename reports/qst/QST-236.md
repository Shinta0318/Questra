## 2026-10-28 Privacy Regression Follow-up

- The planning preference repository previously used one secure-storage key for every account. When the second account had no remote row or the read failed, the first account's local planning conditions and consent flag could be returned. Local storage is now keyed by account ID (with a separate anonymous scope). Tests verify account A's preferences and consent never load for account B or an anonymous session.
- The old unscoped key is deliberately not imported or deleted: its owner cannot be established, and assigning it to a signed-in account could expose another person's conditions. Authenticated users with a remote `weekly_availability` row reload their settings from that owner-scoped row. Existing offline-only settings in the legacy key need user review rather than silent attribution.
- The Quest Planning client previously sent weekly time, budget, location, experience, and constraints as separate request fields even when `approved_context` was withheld for lack of consent. All auxiliary planning fields are now omitted unless planning consent is granted. Quest text deliberately remains governed by the separate AI legal acceptance. Flutter regression tests and analysis passed; a live two-account device drill has not yet been repeated.
